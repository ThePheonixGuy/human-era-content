The East-West Schism, or Great Schism, is the break of communion between the Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church that dates from 1054. Disputes over the Filioque clause of the Nicene Creed, the use of leavened bread and papal authority, along with political tensions, preceded the formal split.
