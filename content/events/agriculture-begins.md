Around 9500 BCE, people in the Fertile Crescent of Southwest Asia began cultivating wild cereals and later herded animals. This Neolithic transition moved many communities from hunting and gathering to farming and settled life, and it arose independently in several other regions.

Why it matters: Food surpluses supported larger populations, specialised work and eventually cities, which shaped nearly every later development.
