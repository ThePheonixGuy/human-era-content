The fall of the Western Roman Empire was the loss of central political control in the West, as the empire failed to enforce its rule and its territory split among successor polities. It is often traced across the hundred years from 376, when Goths fleeing the Huns entered the empire, to 476, when the Germanic general Odoacer deposed the last western emperor, Romulus Augustulus.

Why it matters: Many historians use it to mark the end of the ancient world in Western Europe.
