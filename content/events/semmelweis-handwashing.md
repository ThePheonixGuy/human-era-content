The Hungarian doctor Ignaz Semmelweis showed in 1847 that the fatal childbed fever on his hospital ward could be cut sharply if doctors washed their hands in a chlorine solution. Postpartum infection was common and often fatal in the 19th century. His findings were widely rejected at the time but he is now remembered as the saviour of mothers.
