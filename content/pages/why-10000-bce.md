The Human Era (HE), also called the Holocene calendar, adds exactly 10,000 years to the usual year number. So 1 CE is 10,001 HE and 2026 CE is 12,026 HE. Cesare Emiliani proposed it in 1993, although similar proposals had been made decades earlier.

# Why 10,000 years?

The starting point sits near the beginning of the Holocene epoch and near the Neolithic Revolution, when people began farming. The last ice age gave way to the warm Holocene roughly 11,700 years ago, and the first farms followed. Counting from about 10,000 BCE puts those first steps at the very start of the calendar.

# Why it is handy

- **One era.** All of recorded history sits in a single count with no BCE to CE flip.
- **No year zero trouble.** The CE calendar goes straight from 1 BCE to 1 CE, so subtracting across the border is off by one. In HE, 1 BCE is 10,000 HE and 1 CE is 10,001 HE, and the sums just work.
- **Round and memorable.** Ten thousand is a tidy number. It is a convention, not a measured date: farming did not begin in one exact year.

# Where it came from

Cesare Emiliani (1922 to 1995) was an Italian-American scientist and the founder of paleoceanography. He first suggested aligning the era with the 7,980-year Julian cycle, starting in 4713 BCE (5288 HE), but dropped that plan in favour of the 10,000 year shift.
