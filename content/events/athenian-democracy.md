In 508 BCE the Athenian lawgiver Cleisthenes reformed the constitution of Athens and set it on a democratic footing. Historians call him the father of Athenian democracy. Citizens could take part directly, although women, enslaved people and foreigners were excluded.

Why it matters: Athens gave later ages a working model of citizen self-government and a vocabulary for discussing it.
