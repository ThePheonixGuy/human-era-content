Northern German towns, led by Lübeck, formed trading alliances in the thirteenth century, with a Lübeck and Hamburg agreement in 1241 often seen as an early step. The league grew into a network of merchant guilds and market towns across northern and central Europe. It dominated Baltic commerce for centuries.
