The Atlantic slave trade carried enslaved African people to the Americas on slave ships from both Europe and the New World, in the early phases often along the triangular trade route and its Middle Passage. Europeans set up a coastal slave trade in the 15th century, and large-scale shipments to the Americas ran from the 1500s to 1866. More than twelve million people were shipped.

Why it matters: Its legacies still shape the economies and societies of Africa, the Americas and Europe.
