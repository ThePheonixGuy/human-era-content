The Hemudu culture, about 5500 to 3300 BCE, lived on the eastern coast of China south of Hangzhou Bay. Its people were among the earliest rice farmers, built stilt houses and made black pottery tempered with charcoal. The site was found in 1973.

Why it matters: It documents rice farming in the Yangtze delta thousands of years before the first Chinese states.
