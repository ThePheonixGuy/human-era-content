The Bronze Age is the phase in which societies in Asia, the Near East and Europe produced bronze by alloying copper with tin or arsenic, or traded for it. In the Near East it begins around 3300 BCE. Regions without local tin relied on trade to obtain the metal.

Why it matters: Bronze tools and weapons, and the trade routes that supplied tin, helped drive the rise of states and long-distance commerce.
