Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, a largely self-taught Dutch draper, ground his own lenses and built simple microscopes of great power. In a letter to the Royal Society in 1676 he described tiny living creatures in rain water, now known as microorganisms. He became known as a father of microbiology.
