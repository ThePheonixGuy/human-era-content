Constantine I was Roman emperor from 306 to 337 CE and the first emperor to convert to Christianity. His victory at the Milvian Bridge in 312 preceded his support for the faith, including the Edict of Milan, which ended the persecution of Christians. He founded Constantinople.

Why it matters: Imperial backing turned Christianity from a persecuted minority faith into the dominant religion of the empire.
