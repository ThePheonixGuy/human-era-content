A decade of drought and poor farming practice in the 1930s led to severe dust storms across the High Plains of the United States. The storms damaged farmland in Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, Colorado and New Mexico. Many families left their farms, and the disaster led to new soil conservation programs.
