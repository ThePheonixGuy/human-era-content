The Black Death was a plague pandemic in Europe from 1346 to 1353, caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis and spread mainly by flea bites. It killed up to 50 million people, around 30 to 60 percent of Europe's population and about a third of the Middle East's.

Why it matters: The labour shortages it caused reshaped European economies and society.
