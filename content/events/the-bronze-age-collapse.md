The Late Bronze Age collapse was a period of societal collapse in the Mediterranean basin in the late 13th to early 12th century BCE. It hit Egypt, Anatolia, the Aegean, eastern Libya and the Balkans. The collapse was sudden, violent and culturally disruptive, and the causes are still debated.

Why it matters: It ended the Bronze Age palace economies and opened the way for the Iron Age states.
