Alexander III of Macedon became king in 336 BCE at age 20 and led a long campaign through Asia and Egypt. His defeat of Darius III at Gaugamela in 331 BCE opened the Persian heartland to him. By the age of 30 he ruled one of the largest empires of the ancient world.

Why it matters: His conquests carried Greek language and culture across Western Asia and shaped the Hellenistic world that followed.
