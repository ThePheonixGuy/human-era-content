In June 2012 Jennifer Doudna, Emmanuelle Charpentier and colleagues showed that the bacterial CRISPR-Cas9 system could be programmed to cut DNA at a chosen site. The technique allows scientists to edit the genomes of living organisms. It was recognised with the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
