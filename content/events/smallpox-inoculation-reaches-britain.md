Lady Mary Wortley Montagu promoted inoculation she had seen in the Ottoman Empire, and in 1721 the practice was tried in Britain. In this variolation, material from a smallpox patient gave a milder infection that protected against the disease. Far fewer variolated people died than those who caught smallpox naturally, and the practice was a precursor to vaccination.
