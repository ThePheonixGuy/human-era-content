Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha, was a wandering religious teacher who lived in the eastern Indo-Gangetic Plains in the 6th or 5th century BCE and founded Buddhism. Buddhist tradition says he was born at Lumbini, in present-day Nepal, to a royal family of the Shakya clan. The traditional date is 563 BCE.

Why it matters: Buddhism spread across Asia and became a major world religion.
