Magna Carta is a royal charter of rights sealed by King John of England at Runnymede, near Windsor, on 15 June 1215. Archbishop Stephen Langton drafted it to make peace between the unpopular king and rebel barons. It protected church rights and the barons from illegal imprisonment and arbitrary demands.

Why it matters: It became a lasting symbol that even a king is subject to law.
