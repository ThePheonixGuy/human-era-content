The Code of Hammurabi is a Babylonian legal text composed around 1753 BCE and attributed to Hammurabi, sixth king of the First Dynasty of Babylon. It is written in Old Babylonian Akkadian, and the main copy is inscribed on a basalt stele 2.25 metres tall. Harari reads it as a cooperation manual that ranked people into unequal classes.

Why it matters: It is among the best-known legal texts of the ancient Near East and shows how early states wrote down law.
