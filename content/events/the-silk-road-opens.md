The Silk Road was a network of Asian trade routes active from the second century BCE until the mid-15th century. It spanned over 6,400 kilometres on land and linked the Eastern and Western worlds economically, culturally, politically and religiously. The name was coined in the late 19th century.

Why it matters: Goods, religions, technologies and diseases moved along it between China, Central Asia, Persia and the Mediterranean.
