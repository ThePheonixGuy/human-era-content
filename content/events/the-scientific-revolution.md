The Scientific Revolution of the 16th and 17th centuries in Europe was an irreversible break with earlier natural philosophy. The new science was more mechanistic, more integrated with mathematics and focused on gathering knowledge by observation and experiment. It ran from Copernicus in 1543 to Newton in 1687.

Why it matters: The methods it built underpin modern science.
