Nevalı Çori on the middle Euphrates in Şanlıurfa Province, Turkey, was occupied from about 8400 BCE to 8100 BCE. A Heidelberg team led by Harald Hauptmann dug it from 1983 to 1991. It is known for early temples, monumental limestone sculpture and the oldest domesticated einkorn wheat.

Why it matters: Shrines and farming appear together here, which ties early ritual to the first farmers.
