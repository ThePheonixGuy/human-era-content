Augustus, also called Octavian, founded the Roman Empire and was its first emperor from 27 BCE until his death in 14 CE. His reign began an imperial cult and an era of imperial peace. He established the principate, a form of government in which the emperor showed nominal deference to the Senate.

Why it matters: This system lasted for centuries and ruled the Mediterranean world.
