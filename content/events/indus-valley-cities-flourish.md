The Indus Valley Civilisation was a Bronze Age civilisation in northwestern South Asia lasting from 3300 to 1300 BCE, and in its mature form from 2600 to 1900 BCE. Along with Egypt, Mesopotamia and China it was one of the four centres of the ancient world, and it covered a larger area than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined.

Why it matters: Its planned cities, such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, show that urban life arose independently in South Asia.
