Galileo Galilei was an Italian astronomer and physicist born in Pisa. In 1609 he built a telescope and used it to observe the sky, including the Moon and the moons of Jupiter. He is called the father of observational astronomy.

Why it matters: His observations supported the Copernican view of the cosmos and brought him into conflict with the Church.
