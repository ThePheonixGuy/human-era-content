In the 5th millennium BCE, people at Nabta Playa in the Nubian Desert of southern Egypt set stones in alignments that may have marked the rising of certain stars. A "calendar circle" there indicates the approximate direction of summer solstice sunrise, and it may be among the earliest known archaeoastronomical devices. The date here is approximate.
