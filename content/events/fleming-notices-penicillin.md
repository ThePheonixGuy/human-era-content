Sir Alexander Fleming, a Scottish physician and microbiologist, discovered in 1928 the mould Penicillium rubens producing what was later named benzylpenicillin. It was the first antibiotic substance discovered. He shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Howard Florey and Ernst Chain.

Why it matters: Penicillin opened the age of antibiotics and made once-deadly infections treatable.
