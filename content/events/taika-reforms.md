After a coup ended the power of the Soga clan, Emperor Kōtoku began the Taika Reforms in 645, with formal edicts in 646. They abolished hereditary titles, reorganised the provinces and put land and taxes under central control on the Chinese model.

Why it matters: They began the shift to a Chinese-style state in Japan.
